    About the course#

    Logistics#

    All announcements, materials and submissions are on Google Classroom

    Google Classroom is the single official place for the course.

    Course registration on the UoN student portal is mandatory before you can sit the CAT and the examination

    A student who is not registered cannot be examined, whatever work they have done.

    Attendance is mandatory and is recorded in every class, online and physical

    Attendance is taken in both modes.

    Delivery: blended — some classes online, some physical

    Online classes are on Google Meet; the link is on Google Classroom.

    Group work: all coursework is done in groups of 4–5

    Assignments and the project are group work. Every member must contribute (slide 12).

    This course follows a learner-centred approach

    Students read ahead, run notebooks on their own, present their work and critique each other's. The lecturer guides.

    Summary of course topics#

    The course follows ISLP chapter by chapter, in eight topics.

    1. Introduction: statistical learning, model accuracy, bias–variance trade-off

    Today; ISLP Chapter 2.

    1. Linear regression

    ISLP Chapter 3: fitting a straight line (or plane) by least squares.

    1. Classification

    ISLP Chapter 4: predicting a category, e.g. healthy or faulty.

    1. Resampling and regularisation

    ISLP Chapters 5–6: estimating test error honestly (cross-validation), and controlling complexity.

    1. Non-linear models and trees

    ISLP Chapters 7–8.

    1. Support vector machines

    ISLP Chapter 9.

    1. Neural networks and deep learning

    ISLP Chapter 10.

    1. Unsupervised learning

    ISLP Chapter 12: finding structure without labels.

    Group project: present and defend your results

    At the end of the semester.

    Prerequisites#

    Programming: Python, with NumPy, pandas and Matplotlib

    All 32 have used Python; 29 have used these libraries.

    Mathematics: linear algebra, calculus, probability and statistics

    Data are matrices (slides 21–23); training means minimising an error (derivatives); noise needs probability.

    Physics and instrumentation: sensors, signals and measurement

    The examples come from the students' own field.

    Problem-solving skills, and enthusiasm to explore new topics

    The most important prerequisite.

    Expected learning outcomes#

    By the end of the course, you should be able to: …

    These are the objectives of the SPM 405 outline. Assessment is designed around them: explaining (examination), applying in Python (assignments), choosing and justifying methods, building and evaluating models (project), and critical evaluation (presentation).

    Reading materials#

    Main texts (both on Google Classroom)

    Two books by the same Stanford group, written to be read together. Both PDFs are posted on Google Classroom.

    1. James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani and Taylor, An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in Python (ISLP), Springer (2023). The course follows this book chapter by chapter

    ISLP explains each method with little mathematics and ends every chapter with a Python lab. The eight course topics (slide 7) are its Chapters 2–10 and 12. Today's lecture is Chapter 2.

    1. Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman, The Elements of Statistical Learning: Data Mining, Inference, and Prediction, 2nd ed., Springer (2009). The same topics in more mathematical depth

    ESL is the advanced companion of ISLP: the same methods with full derivations. Students use it when they want the mathematics behind a result, for example the bias–variance decomposition (ESL Section 7.3).

    Python refresher: Python for Complete Beginners

    The course site; sections 1–6 are already set as self-study.

    Course ethics (UoN rules and regulations)#

    Attend all classes and assessments

    As on slide 6.

    Keep time during class activities; they are timed

    So that every group gets its turn.

    Refrain from cheating, plagiarism or any other irregularity

    Plagiarism is presenting someone else's work, words or code as one's own.

    Examination irregularities are dealt with under the University of Nairobi regulations

    Students must know these rules.

    Every group member must contribute and be able to explain the group's work

    Any member may be asked to explain any part.

    Do not upload or share any course materials

    The materials are for registered students only.

    AI policy#

    SPM 405 AI policy

    The same wording students accepted in the survey. AI assistants are tools for learning, not a replacement for the student's own work.

    AI assistants may be used for exploration, brainstorming, concept clarification and debugging

    For example, asking for a concept in different words, or help finding a bug.

    You must be able to explain every line of code you submit

    The test of honest use.

    AI-generated content must be acknowledged and critically checked

    AI assistants make confident mistakes.

    Violations are handled under the University of Nairobi plagiarism policy

    Like any other academic misconduct.
