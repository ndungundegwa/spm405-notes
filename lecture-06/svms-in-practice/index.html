    SVMs in practice#

    More than two classes#

    One-versus-one: fit an SVM for every pair of \(K\) classes: \(K(K - 1)/2\) SVMs. Each votes; the class with the most votes wins. (scikit-learn's SVC does this.)

    One-versus-all: fit \(K\) SVMs: each class against all the others. Choose the class whose SVM is most confident

    Fault type: three classes (imbalance, misalignment, bearing wear): 3 SVMs either way

    \(3 \times 2/2 = 3\) pairs; and 3 one-versus-all SVMs. With 10 classes it would be 45 against 10.

    Symbols

    \(K\)Number of classes.

    SVMs and logistic regression#

    Plot: hinge loss (SVM) and log loss (logistic) against \(y_if(x_i)\), the signed distance

    \(y_if(x_i)\) is positive for a correctly classified point and large when it is far on the correct side. The SVM can be written as minimising the hinge loss \(\max(0, 1 - y_if(x_i))\) plus a ridge-like penalty (ISLP 9.5).

    Both penalise points on the wrong side. The SVM ignores points safely outside the street (loss exactly 0)

    That is why only support vectors matter. The log loss is never exactly zero, so every point has a little influence.

    The name: the graph is bent like a door hinge at \(y_if(x_i) = 1\).

    So the two methods usually give similar answers: 0.177 and 0.176 on dataset B

    Logistic regression gives probabilities; an SVM gives a class (and a distance)

    Things to watch#

    Standardise the inputs: kernels use distances

    Tune \(C\) and \(\gamma\) together by cross-validation, over a grid

    They interact: a large \(\gamma\) needs a smaller \(C\) to avoid overfitting.

    Know your library's \(C\): scikit-learn's \(C\) is the opposite of ISLP's

    Large datasets: kernel SVMs become slow beyond some tens of thousands of rows

    Training time grows roughly with the square of the number of rows or worse.

    Probabilities: SVC(probability=True) estimates them with an extra step; they are less reliable than logistic regression's

    The extra step fits a sigmoid to the SVM distances using internal cross-validation (Platt scaling).

    Summary#

    1. The maximal margin classifier finds the widest street between two classes
    1. Only the support vectors decide the boundary
    1. The soft margin allows overlap; \(C\) controls the width (in scikit-learn: small \(C\) = wide)
    1. Kernels give curved boundaries by enlarging the feature space implicitly
    1. SVMs and logistic regression often perform alike; kernels win when the boundary is curved
