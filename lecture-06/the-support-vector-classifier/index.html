    The support vector classifier#

    When the classes overlap#

    Real data overlap: no line separates them perfectly

    Then the maximal margin classifier has no solution.

    Support vector classifier: allow some points inside the street, or on the wrong side of the line, but pay a price for each one

    Each point gets a slack variable \(\varepsilon_i\): 0 if it is on the correct side outside the street, between 0 and 1 if inside the street, above 1 if on the wrong side (ISLP 9.2.2).

    Support vectors are now all points on the edge, inside the street, or on the wrong side

    Also called the soft margin classifier

    Why it helps: even separable data are often better with a soft margin: one odd sample should not decide the line

    Because only the support vectors matter, a single unusual sample near the other class can swing a hard-margin line.

    Symbols

    \(\varepsilon_i\)Slack of point \(i\): how far it violates the margin.

    The cost parameter \(C\): two conventions!#

    ISLP: \(C\) is a budgetscikit-learn: \(C\) is a penalty
    large \(C\)many violations allowed: wide streetviolations are expensive: narrow street
    small \(C\)few violations: narrow streetviolations are cheap: wide street

    ISLP: \(C\) is a budget: large \(C\): many violations allowed: wide street; small \(C\): few violations: narrow street

    In ISLP (equation 9.15), the total slack must satisfy \(\sum_i\varepsilon_i \le C\): \(C\) is how much violation we can afford.

    scikit-learn: \(C\) is a penalty: large \(C\): violations are expensive: narrow street; small \(C\): violations are cheap: wide street

    scikit-learn minimises \(\frac12\Vert w\Vert ^2 + C\sum_i\varepsilon_i\): \(C\) is the price per unit of violation.

    Same idea, opposite direction. In this course we use scikit-learn: small \(C\) = wide street, more support vectors, lower variance, higher bias

    A wide street is decided by many points, so it changes little between datasets (low variance) but may not follow the data closely (high bias).

    Beyond the slide: a common mistake

    Students (and textbooks!) mix up the two. Reading “large \(C\) gives a wide margin” from ISLP and applying it in scikit-learn gives the opposite of what was intended. Always check which convention a library uses.

    All with 200 training samples and 2000 test samples.

    Small \(C\): wide street, 150 of 200 points are support vectors

    With a street that wide, most points are inside it.

    From \(C = 1\) upwards the answer hardly changes

    Once violations are expensive enough, the line is set by the points in the overlap region, and more expense changes nothing.

    Logistic regression on the same data: test error 0.176

    The best SVM and logistic regression are equal here (slide 19 explains why).

    Beyond the slide: what CV says

    The 10-fold CV errors are 0.135, 0.150, 0.160, 0.160, 0.165 for \(C = 0.01\) to 100: CV would choose \(C = 0.01\), which is the worst on the test data. With 200 samples, one misclassification changes a CV error by 0.005, so these differences are within the noise (notebook question 3). A lesson from Lecture 4: small CV differences are not meaningful.

    Dataset B: two lines#

    Green (\(C = 0.01\)): many points pull on the line; it shifts. Red (\(C = 100\)): the line is set by the points near the overlap

    The two lines have similar slopes but different positions. With 150 support vectors, the green line is pulled towards the bulk of the data.

    Choose \(C\) by cross-validation, as \(\lambda\) in Lecture 4
