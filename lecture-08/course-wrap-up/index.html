    Choosing a method#

    What is your output?

    • A number: regression.
    • A category: classification.
    • None: PCA or clustering.

    What are your inputs?

    • A table of measured features: linear or logistic regression, the lasso, random forests, boosting.
    • Images: a convolutional network.
    • Sequences: an autoregressive model, a recurrent network.

    Always:

    1. start with a simple baseline (guess the average, or the most common class; then a linear or logistic regression);
    2. compare methods by cross-validation;
    3. test once, at the end, on data never used for any choice;
    4. check that the result makes physical sense.

    A checklist for your own project#

    When you apply these methods to a problem of your own, write up:

    1. the problem, and why it matters;
    2. the data: source, size, inputs, output;
    3. the methods you compared, with a table of 10-fold cross-validation errors (and the baseline);
    4. your best model, and what it tells you about the problem;
    5. its limitations, and what you would do next.

    Summary of Lecture 8#

    1. Unsupervised learning finds structure without an output to predict.
    2. PCA summarises many correlated variables with a few components; the scree plot shows how many to keep.
    3. The PCA reconstruction error detects readings that break the normal pattern.
    4. \(k\)-means and hierarchical clustering find groups; scaling decides what “similar” means.
    5. Clusters are a starting point: check that they are stable and meaningful.
